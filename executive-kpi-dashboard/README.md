# Executive KPI Dashboard & Business Performance Analysis

## Dashboard Purpose

This executive dashboard provides leadership with a high-level view of company performance, including revenue, profitability, growth trends, and segment performance. It is designed to support strategic decision-making by highlighting both performance drivers and areas requiring attention.

## Business Scenario
Executives need a high-level view of company performance to make strategic decisions. This project simulates an executive dashboard that tracks revenue, profit, customer growth, and operational efficiency metrics.

## Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)
This dashboard focuses on metrics commonly used by leadership:
- Total Revenue
- Profit Margin
- Monthly Sales Growth
- Customer Acquisition Trend
- Top Performing Products or Regions
- Underperforming Segments

## KPI Highlights
- Overall profit margin is 30%
- Revenue shows downward trends over time
- Customer count is decreasing over time
- Revenue Growth Is Consistently Negative
- Electronical products drive the most revenue, and Beauty products has low profitability.

## Key Business Insights
- Monthly profit closely mirrors the revenue trend, indicating that profitability is largely driven by sales volume. When revenue increases, profit rises proportionally, suggesting relatively stable profit margins over time.
- Noticeable profit peaks occur in certain months (e.g., mid-year and year-end), suggesting seasonal demand patterns or successful promotional periods.
-  Some months show clear declines in profit, despite the overall stable trend. Possible causes include lower sales volume, higher discounting, or increased operational costs.
-  Because profit and revenue trends move together, profit margins appear relatively stable rather than highly volatile.
-   The final month shows a significant drop in profit compared to prior months.
-   Since margins are stable and no product is underperforming dramatically, the primary strategic challenge is revenue growth, not cost control.
   
## Business Value
- The company’s cost structure appears consistent, with no major margin shocks during high- or low-sales months.
- High-performing months should be supported with adequate inventory, staffing, and marketing efforts to maximize returns.
- Periods with profit dips require deeper investigation to identify whether margin erosion is driven by pricing strategies or rising costs.
- Long-term profit growth will depend more on increasing sales volume and market expansion than on margin improvements alone.
- The sharp drop in the most recent month may indicate incomplete reporting, seasonality, or a potential downturn. Leadership should verify data completeness and assess whether corrective action is needed.
- The business model is stable, but growth momentum is weakening. Strategy should prioritize demand generation, market expansion, or product innovation rather than margin restructuring.
